## Set a useful agenda

A discovery call should tell both sides whether a project makes sense. Open by confirming the available time and the decision you need to reach.

> We have twenty-five minutes. I’d like to understand what is happening now, what you want to change, and whether I am a good fit. If I am, I’ll explain the next step before we finish.

This makes the call easier to manage and gives you permission to say when the work sits outside your skills.

## Ask about the present first

Avoid racing toward a solution. Ask questions that uncover the current process, the people involved, and the cost of leaving it alone:

- What prompted you to look at this now?
- How do customers complete this task today?
- Where do they get stuck or ask for help?
- Who needs to review and approve the work?
- Is there a date driving the project? What happens if it moves?
- What has already been tried?

Ask one question, then listen. Write down the client’s exact nouns and verbs. If they call a booking a “reservation,” use that term in your scope. Pause rather than filling every silence with advice.

When an answer stays abstract, ask for a recent example: “Can you walk me through the last enquiry that went wrong?” A real incident exposes steps that a general statement hides.

## Notice bad fit early

A difficult fit often appears as a process problem before it becomes a personality problem. Watch for a missing decision-maker, an immovable deadline with no available content, unclear ownership, or a request to copy another company’s work. Treat insults toward previous freelancers and pressure to begin without written terms as reasons to decline.

Do not diagnose the client on the call. State the practical constraint: “I cannot meet that date if approval takes two weeks,” or “I do not reproduce another studio’s design.”

Close by summarising the problem, desired outcome, constraints, and next action. Ask the client to correct your summary. If the fit is sound, promise a proposal by a named day. If it is not, say so promptly instead of keeping an unsuitable lead warm.
