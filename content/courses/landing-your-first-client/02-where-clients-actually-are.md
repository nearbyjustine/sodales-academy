## Start with trust already in the room

Your first client is more likely to come through a shared context than a crowded listing. Make a list of people who have seen you work: former classmates, colleagues, relatives with businesses, community organisers, and people from hobby groups. You are not asking them to buy. You are giving them a clear sentence they can repeat.

> I build small, fast websites for local service businesses. I have room for one project this month, so please introduce me if someone mentions an outdated or broken site.

“I do web development” is too broad to remember. The tighter version names the buyer, the problem, and your availability.

## Three places to look

Use a simple prospect sheet and add names from these sources:

- **Referrals:** Ask people who know your work for an introduction, not a vague recommendation. A three-person message gives you a warm start.
- **Local businesses:** Walk through the buying journey before contacting anyone. Check the site on your phone, find the opening hours, and try the enquiry path without submitting it.
- **Communities:** Contribute where business owners or other freelancers already talk. Answer a question or review a page when invited. Repeated useful contact builds recognition.

Record the business, contact, shared context, observed issue, last action, and follow-up date. Ten researched names are more useful than a hundred scraped addresses.

## Put job boards last

Job boards can help you learn how clients describe work, but each listing invites direct comparison on price, ratings, and platform history. A newcomer enters that comparison with little evidence. Apply when the brief closely matches work you can show, and write to the brief rather than sending a reusable biography.

Set a limit, such as two careful applications per week. Spend the rest of your outreach time on introductions, local research, and community participation. Those routes let a client judge how you think before comparing a row of bids.

For your first prospect list, choose five warm contacts, five local businesses, and two community conversations. Add one honest next action beside every name. “Research later” is not an action; “check the mobile booking path on Thursday” is.
