## Reflect the discovery conversation

A proposal records the decision the client is being asked to make. Open with the current problem, the desired outcome, and any constraints confirmed during discovery. Use the client’s language and keep this section short enough that they can correct a misunderstanding.

Then state your recommended approach. Explain the phases and why they fit the problem, without turning the document into a tutorial about your tools.

## Make scope testable

List deliverables as objects or outcomes someone can verify. “Website design” is loose. A clearer section reads:

```text
Included
- One responsive homepage design
- Three reusable page templates: Service, About, Contact
- Implementation in the existing Next.js repository
- Two consolidated feedback rounds
- Browser and keyboard testing before handoff

Client supplies
- Approved copy and photographs by 12 November
- One decision-maker for consolidated feedback
```

Add exclusions where a reasonable person might assume they are included, such as copywriting, photography, paid fonts, hosting fees, or data entry. Do not pad the exclusions with remote possibilities.

## Connect dates, money, and decisions

Show the sequence rather than promising a finish date in isolation. Name the start condition, milestones, review windows, client dependencies, and what happens when feedback arrives late. Include the fee, currency, tax treatment where relevant, payment schedule, proposal expiry date, and ownership or licensing terms.

> Work begins after the signed agreement and booking payment. Dates move by the same number of business days when required client material or consolidated feedback arrives late.

The proposal should also describe the change process: you will document any request outside scope, quote its cost and schedule effect, and wait for approval before starting it.

## Remove decision friction

End with one acceptance route. Name the document to sign, the first invoice, and the person to contact with questions. Check names, dates, arithmetic, links, and deliverables before sending. Ask someone unfamiliar with the project to read the scope; if they cannot tell what will exist at handoff, revise it before the client has to ask.
