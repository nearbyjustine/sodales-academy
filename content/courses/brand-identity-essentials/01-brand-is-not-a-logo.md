## Monday: “We need a cleaner logo”

Nia runs a small accounting firm. Her brief asks for a cleaner logo before tax season. During the first conversation, she also says that shop owners often contact the firm after missing a filing date. They send records from their phones after closing and worry that an accountant will scold them for being disorganised.

A new logo may identify Nia’s firm more clearly, but it cannot decide what the firm promises those shop owners. The designer pauses the sketching and writes down the clues already on the table:

- Audience: shop owners handling records outside business hours
- Trigger: a deadline, warning, or pile of unfinished records
- Alternative: doing it alone or asking a general bookkeeper
- Risk: feeling judged, confused, or surprised by the price

## The diagnosis

Nia’s current website says, “Professional solutions for businesses that value quality.” Any accounting firm could publish that line. It gives a writer no useful vocabulary and gives a photographer no scene to capture.

The discovery notes point toward a sharper position: practical filing help for owners who need calm, after-hours guidance. That choice could change the order of the homepage, the examples in a service page, response-time information, and the tone of an overdue-document reminder.

## Positioning draft, marked up

```text
For shop owners [a group we can recognise]
who need to get late or untidy records under control [the situation],
Nia & Co. provides bookkeeping and filing support [the category].
Clients receive a plain checklist and evening document drop-off [credible difference].
```

This internal statement still needs checking against real sales conversations and repeated customer questions. It should never promise an evening service if the firm cannot provide one.

## Friday: the voice test

The team chooses “calm,” “plain-spoken,” and “respectful” as voice traits, then tests them on a difficult message.

> We are still missing your March sales record. Send it through the secure folder by Thursday, or call Mara so we can agree on another date.

That message explains the gap, the action, and the fallback. The same voice can guide a homepage, an invoice note, and a complaint reply. Now the logo has a job inside a business system instead of carrying the whole brand by itself.
