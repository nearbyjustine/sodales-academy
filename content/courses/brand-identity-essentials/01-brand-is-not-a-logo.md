## Decide what the business should mean

A logo is one identifier inside a larger system. Customers also meet the business through its offer, language, photographs, pricing, service, and follow-up. If those parts make conflicting promises, a polished mark cannot repair the experience.

Start with positioning. Write down the specific audience, the situation that brings them to the business, the available alternatives, and the reason they might choose this offer. Avoid claims such as “for everyone who values quality.” They do not help a writer choose a headline or a designer choose an image.

Use a working statement:

```text
For [specific audience] who need [job or change],
[business] offers [category or service].
They choose it over [real alternative] because [credible difference].
```

This is an internal decision tool, not homepage copy. Test each blank against evidence from customer interviews, sales conversations, reviews, or repeated questions.

## Describe an audience you can picture

Useful audience notes cover behavior and context rather than a fictional biography. A small accounting firm might serve shop owners who manage records after closing, use a phone more than a laptop, and worry about missing filing dates. Those facts affect content order, channel choice, and support.

Ask:

- What event makes this person look for help?
- What have they already tried?
- Which words do they use for the problem?
- Who else influences the decision?
- What would make the offer feel risky?

## Set boundaries for the voice

Choose three voice traits and define how each one behaves. “Friendly” alone is open to interpretation. “Direct: short sentences, plain prices, no teasing headlines” gives a copywriter something to apply.

> Calm means we explain the next step and avoid urgency. It does not mean formal legal language or vague reassurance.

Add a counterexample for each trait. A confident voice might state recommendations without boasting; it should not guarantee outcomes the business cannot control. Draft a complaint reply, a price increase notice, and an ordinary service description. If the voice only works in a campaign headline, the system is not ready for the daily work of the business.
