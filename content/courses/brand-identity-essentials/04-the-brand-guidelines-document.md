## Write for the next person doing the work

Brand guidelines help a client make routine decisions after the designer leaves. The likely readers are staff preparing a social post, a printer checking a file, a developer building a page, or a partner placing the logo beside other marks. Organise the document around those jobs.

Begin with a short foundation: audience, positioning, voice traits, and the main promise. Then document the assets and decisions people will apply:

- Logo files, minimum size, clear space, and approved backgrounds
- Colour names, values, roles, and tested combinations
- Typefaces, licenses, hierarchy, and fallback fonts
- Photography or illustration principles with usable examples
- Voice guidance across ordinary and difficult messages
- Contact and version information

## Show failure beside success

A rule becomes clearer when the reader can compare an approved use with a plausible mistake. Show the logo at the minimum readable size beside one that is too small. Show a correct colour pair beside a low-contrast pair. Explain why the rejected version fails instead of filling a page with crossed-out decorations.

```text
Use: Violet for links on paper after checking the intended size and state.
Avoid: Pale lilac for body text on paper; the pair is too faint for reading.
```

Include downloadable assets with names that identify their purpose, such as `logo-horizontal-dark.svg` and `logo-mark-white.png`. “Final-final-2” tells the client nothing six months later.

## Design the handoff

Ask how the client creates real materials and provide guidance in that format. A team working in presentation software needs editable templates and font fallbacks. A developer needs variables and exportable assets. A printer needs colour specifications and vector artwork.

> The document is a map; the asset folder is the equipment. Deliver both, and make sure every link works from the client’s account.

Finish with a thirty-minute handoff using a real task. Ask the client to create a sample post or choose the correct logo for a dark background while you watch. Their questions reveal missing labels and ambiguous rules. Correct the document, add its date and version, and state who can approve exceptions.
